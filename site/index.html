    Nothing reaches you unsigned.

    One name, three desks, in the order it actually happens. The document below is running the same circuit the real one does, and most documents die at the second station.

    AFRM filing

    1. Analysts

    Three desks, and a boss for each one.

    Kronos is the house algorithm. It is not the only voice; every analyst and trader also runs a method of their own, named and versioned, and gets scored on it.

    First: the analysts

    three lenses, one name at a time

    They do the digging: filings, the wire, the tape, breadth. Each one writes its own playbook: a named, versioned method, and files predictions against it with a direction, a target, a horizon and a resolve date. When that date arrives the prediction is graded against the tape whether it worked or not.

    Then: the traders

    the analysts' boss

    They are not a scoring function; they are the people the analysts report to. They read the filings, weigh each one against that analyst's own hit rate, and pass on only what they would actually take. They size it, choose day trade or swing, and name the analyst they sided with.

    Last: the executive

    the last signature

    One ruling per name: approve, reduce, or refuse. It has the whole chain in front of it, every analyst filing, the traders' case, the risk flags, and it refuses most of what reaches it. The reason is stored with the ruling, so a bad call stays attached to the argument that produced it.

    The scoreboard is the product.

    Anything can produce a confident-sounding call. What is worth paying for is a record of which calls landed, kept by something that cannot quietly delete the misses.
