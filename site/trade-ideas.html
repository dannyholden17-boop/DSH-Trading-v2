Five parts. None of them optional.

If any one of these is missing, the idea doesn't ship. The last one matters most: every idea carries a date it gets graded on, so the feed cannot quietly forget the calls that went wrong.

1
The thesis

One paragraph, plain English, naming the specific thing that happened: the sweep, the filing, the level reclaimed. If it can't be written in a sentence, it isn't understood.

2
The confidence score

How far the desk agreed with itself, after the traders weigh each filing against that analyst's own record. It measures conviction in the pattern, never the odds of making money.

3
The numbers

Entry zone, invalidation stop, target and the resulting reward-to-risk. The stop is the honest part: it's the price at which the thesis is simply wrong.

4
The resolve date

Every idea carries the date its own thesis has to be right by. On that date it is graded against the tape and the result is filed, including the ones that were wrong. Nothing here is backtested: the record is built forward, one call at a time.

5
The risk gate

Position size against your book, correlation with what you already hold, and total exposure. An idea that breaks your rules never reaches the feed.

Illustrative example · hypothetical figures

From raw signal to a number you can argue with.

Confidence isn't a vibe. It's the output of four steps, and you can open any of them to see why a score is 88 and not 62.

01

Signal

One of the three analysts files on something concrete: a sweep, a breakout, a filing, a level reclaimed. Raw, unranked, and far too noisy to trade on its own.

02

Cross-check

The other two lenses are asked whether they see it too, without being shown the first one's filing. A tape signal with a catalyst underneath reads very differently from one standing alone.

03

The cut

The traders, the analysts' boss, read all three filings and pass on only what they would put size behind. Most ideas die here, and what died is on the record too.

04

The ruling

The executive approves it, cuts the size, or refuses outright, and says which trader it sided with. Only then does the idea reach you.

A high conviction figure means the desks agreed with each other. It is not a probability of profit, not a guarantee, and not a substitute for your own judgement. No idea here has been backtested — predictions are graded forward, against the tape, once they reach their resolve date.

The fair questions.

Do you place trades for me?

Not unless you explicitly set that up. By default Flux researches and surfaces ideas, and you decide what happens next: paper first, if you want. If you do enable automation, every order still passes the Risk engine's guardrails, and you can stop it at any time.

What does the confidence score actually mean?

It measures how far the desk agreed with itself on a setup, after the traders weigh it against historical outcomes. High confidence means the pattern is consistent and corroborated. It is explicitly not a probability of profit and not a measure of how much you might make.

Are these signals guaranteed?

No. Nothing in markets is, and any product that says otherwise is lying to you. Ideas can be wrong, data can lag, and patterns break. That's why every idea carries a stop, a resolve date and the filing analyst's own graded record, so you can size the risk you're actually taking.

Is this financial advice?

No. Flux is research and automation software. It is not a broker-dealer, not an investment adviser, and nothing it produces is personalised advice or a recommendation. Every decision, and its outcome, is yours.

Where do the entry, stop and target come from?

From the structure of the setup: the level that triggered the signal, the price that would invalidate the thesis, and the next level the tape has historically respected. They're a starting frame to adjust to your own plan, not instructions.

Can I test an idea before risking anything?

Yes, and we'd rather you did. Every idea has a one-tap path to paper trading, on every plan including the free one, and you can watch it resolve without a dollar at risk.
