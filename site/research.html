Every claim gets a date it has to be right by.

Research that can’t be checked is just prose. Flux does not backtest — there is no historical replay engine here, and a number invented for a panel would be worse than none. What exists instead is a forward record: every prediction is filed with the date it resolves, and on that date it is graded against the tape whether it worked or not.

What the research is, and isn't.

Is this investment advice?

No. Flux is research and automation software, not a broker-dealer and not an adviser. Reports summarise public information, model output and your own portfolio data. Nothing in them is a recommendation, and every decision, with its outcome, is yours.

Where does the underlying information come from?

Public filings, earnings transcripts, licensed market data and news feeds, plus the portfolio data you connect. Each claim in a report carries its source, so you can open the filing or the print behind it rather than taking the summary on faith.

Can the AI get it wrong?

Yes. Language models misread things, data feeds lag, and history is not a promise. That's exactly why every claim carries its source, and why every prediction carries the date it has to be right by and is graded against the tape when that date arrives — so you can check the work instead of trusting the tone.

Can I ask for a report on something specific?

Any time. Ask for a name, a sector, a theme or your own book in plain English and the Reporter assembles it on demand, using the same inputs as the scheduled briefs.

How current is a report?

Filings and wire items typically land in the research index within a minute of publication; scheduled reports are written at their configured time. Every report is stamped with the moment it was generated and the cutoff of the data behind it.

Does Flux backtest, and does its record mean I'll make money?

Flux does not backtest: there is no historical replay engine in this product, and any backtested figure you saw here would have been invented. The record it does keep is forward — dated predictions graded against the tape as they come due — and it is a record of a simulated book, so it is hypothetical too. It shows what the desk called and how those calls landed, not what your own fills, timing or risk tolerance would have produced, and not what happens next.
