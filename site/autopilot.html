Every call walks the same five steps.

Nothing skips a desk and nothing reaches you until the last one has signed it, including the calls that get thrown out. Each step is written down.

01

Pick the names

The round opens on a slice of the desk's universe, with the tape's own rating and anything that crossed the wire on those names attached.

02

Three analysts file

Fundamentals, catalyst and tape. Each works one lens, files a direction, a target and a date it can be scored on, and never sees the other two.

03

Their boss cuts

The traders read every filing against that analyst's own hit rate and pass on only what they would put size behind. Most filings die here.

04

The executive rules

Approve, cut the size, or refuse, with the reason and which trader it sided with written down. It refuses far more than it approves.

05

The round is filed

Every filing, ruling and reason is stored. When a prediction reaches its date it is graded against the tape, and the grade goes on the scoreboard.

Model output can be wrong, and a filing is a hypothesis rather than a recommendation. Everything described here happens inside a simulated account with virtual money.

It never trades quietly.

Every market hour and every close, Flux writes up what it did and why. The transparency is the point; you should be able to read a full day of decisions before you trust it with a cent.

What is actually running.

The trade log is the last step. Behind it, six specialised models each answer one narrow question, and the fund only acts when enough of them agree.

These are the models in the loop, named for what runs. Output is probabilistic and can be wrong; it is not a prediction you should act on, and it drives a simulated account only.

Guardrails you set, and can pull anytime.

The paper fund runs on its own. Your real account never does unless you say so, on rules you write, with a stop button that's always yours.

Never without approval

Flux never touches your real account until you explicitly opt in. Until then it only trades its own simulated paper money.

You set the rules

Max position size, daily loss limit, allowed tickers, sectors to avoid; you write the mandate and Autopilot trades inside it.

Pause or stop anytime

One tap freezes new trades or flattens the book. There is no lock-in and no waiting, control is always in your hands.

Every action is logged

Each decision, fill and reason is written down and reported hourly and daily. Nothing happens off the record.

Hard risk gates

The Risk engine blocks oversized, over-correlated or over-exposed trades before they're placed — on paper and, if enabled, on your account.

Kill-switch on drawdown

Hit your daily loss limit and Autopilot halts itself automatically, then tells you why. The floor is a rule, not a hope.

The fair questions.

Is this real money?

No. The Flux Fund is a simulated $10,000 paper account using virtual money. It exists as a transparency tool, a live demonstration of how the AI thinks and trades, not as a real portfolio. Every number you see is hypothetical.

Will it trade MY account?

Only if you opt in and set the rules. By default Autopilot trades nothing but its own paper money. If you connect a brokerage, you define the mandate, max size, loss limits, allowed tickers, and you can pause or stop it at any moment.

Can I see every trade?

Yes. The live log shows each fill as it happens, and the hourly and daily reports write up what the fund did and why. The whole point of the paper account is that nothing is hidden.

Is this financial advice?

No. Flux is research and automation software, not a broker-dealer and not an investment adviser. Nothing it produces is personalised advice or a recommendation, and every decision on your real account is yours.

What strategies does it use?

A multi-engine mix: momentum and breakout scanning, options flow, catalyst-driven setups and mean-reversion; each signal cross-checked and then risk-gated for size, stop, correlation and exposure before it's allowed to trade.

Can it lose money?

Yes. The simulated results include losing trades and losing days, because that's honest. Trading involves risk of loss, including loss of principal, and no strategy, human or AI, removes it.
